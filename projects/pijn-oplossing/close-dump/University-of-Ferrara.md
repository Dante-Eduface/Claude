### 2026-07-10 | meeting | Reconnecting - Eduface × University of Ferrara | id=acti_PhW6W35sRhwAyft7j0xReh1sZlAXSUm5MPuo6DcpuQE
Deelnemers: Filippo Carnevali, dante.torbed@eduface.me, jeroen.van.gessel@eduface.me
SUMMARY:
- Discussed University of Ferrara's recent organizational restructuring that split AI initiatives into a separate department with independent decision-making authority over technology adoption.
- Confirmed writing assignments remain central to university assessment while current approaches to AI integrity rely on professor oversight and student guidelines rather than detection tools.
- Presented Eduface's AI Integrity Checker product, which uses rapid AI-conducted oral exams to verify student knowledge and learning outcomes rather than assign marks.
- Demonstrated integrated learning analytics vision combining multiple AI tools to build student learning profiles and enable personalized learning interventions.
- Agreed to arrange meeting with Professor Fabrizio Rigutsi from the newly established AI center to discuss technology adoption, with response expected after summer break.
### University of Ferrara's Organizational Structure and Responsibilities
- Filippo Carnevali confirmed that writing assignments remain central to assessment and evaluation at the University of Ferrara, appearing in both coursework and final exams.
- The university recently reorganized its artificial intelligence initiatives into a separate, self-contained department split from e-learning and digital learning.
- Filippo now operates in the e-learning and digital learning division while AI evaluation responsibilities have been delegated to colleagues in the new AI department.
- The new AI department acts as a filter and evaluation body, with decision-making authority over technology adoption, pilot organization, and budget allocation.
- Filippo can flag software to the AI department but cannot make final adoption decisions independently.
### Current Approaches to Academic Integrity and AI Usage
- The university has not adopted strict guidelines or AI detector tools, as professors decided these approaches have inherent limitations with rapidly evolving AI models.
- Some professors conduct in-person classroom assessments with laptops to oversee student work directly during assignments.
- Other professors provide guardrails and guidelines encouraging students to use AI transparently while building trust and accountability through academic contracts.
- The university relies on existing academic integrity principles rather than new technological enforcement mechanisms.
- Writing assignments remain central because they combat potential brain rot from AI overuse and are considered superior to oral exams for scaling to large student populations.
- Students are increasingly interested in AI technologies for receiving initial feedback on written assignments.
### Eduface's AI Integrity Checker Product
- Dante introduced Eduface's AI Integrity Checker, designed to verify students understand assignment content through rapid oral examinations with an AI agent.
- The product has students answer specific questions in 30 seconds to one minute, allowing assessment of knowledge rather than authorship.
- Pilot testing with small groups has shown students prepare thoroughly when aware of oral examinations, encouraging genuine learning.
- The tool generates a knowledge score and summary but does not assign academic marks in traditional grading formats.
- Professors can deploy the tool to large cohorts (e.g., 200 students) and review aggregated results rather than individual assessments.
- The product functions as a learning incentive tool rather than a marking mechanism.
### Learning Analytics and Personalized Learning Vision
- Filippo highlighted the absence of learning analytics from current AI tools, making it difficult to identify which topics students struggle with or need clarification.
- Dante demonstrated Eduface's integrated approach combining the AI paper grader, AI integrity checker, and AI exam grader to build individual student learning profiles.
- The learning profile system would enable anonymized analytics showing which topics students prepare for, time spent on preparation, and identified knowledge gaps.
- Eduface envisions using learning profiles to create personalized learning experiences by grouping students with similar knowledge gaps and delivering targeted lessons.
- Filippo expressed interest in the concept but requested practical interface demonstrations to evaluate usability and functionality before committing to adoption.
### University Readiness and Technology Adoption Concerns
- Filippo noted professors remain suspicious of AI-based assessment software, viewing it as controversial and not yet widely accepted at the university.
- The university is still in the awareness phase, training professors on artificial intelligence fundamentals rather than advanced applications.
- Technology evolution has shifted from AI content generation (2025) to feedback and assessment, but local universities lag in adoption sensibility.
- The university acknowledges technology capability for assisted assessment exists but institutional readiness and professor confidence require further development.
- Large institutional change toward AI-assisted assessment requires cultural and training shifts beyond technology availability.
### Next Steps and Contact with AI Department
- Filippo confirmed the new AI department was established only one week prior, with leadership positions still being finalized.
- Dante identified Professor Fabrizio Rigutsi as a potential contact, likely leading the AI center, though the formal AI department head had not yet been appointed.
- The university maintains both administrative and academic organizational structures, with the new AI department likely drawing staff from the academic AI center.
- Filippo agreed to contact Professor Rigutsi to explore interest in meeting with Eduface and to assess technology scouting priorities.
- Filippo will provide feedback within days but noted summer vacation in Italy may delay responses until August or September.
