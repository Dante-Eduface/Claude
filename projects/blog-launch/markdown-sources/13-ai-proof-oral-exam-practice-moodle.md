# AI-Proof Oral Exam Practice in Moodle: How Students Rehearse Vivas Before They Count

*By Eduface Team · July 2026 · 8 min read*

Oral exams are having a moment, and most of the coverage points to the same reason: a written assignment is easy to produce with AI, a live conversation about your own work still isn't. Our existing piece on AI oral exams in Moodle looks at using that format specifically to verify authorship. This one is about the other half of making an AI-proof oral exam actually work well in practice: letting students rehearse it first, as many times as they need, before the one attempt that counts.

## Why rehearsal matters more than most syllabi admit

Oral assessment is unusual in higher education because there's normally exactly one attempt. A written exam, a student can draft, redraft, and submit when it's ready. A viva happens once, live, and a nervous or under-prepared answer on the day is the answer that gets graded, however well the student actually understands the material.

There's a well established finding in cognitive psychology that's directly relevant here, even though it was discovered studying word lists rather than oral exams. Henry Roediger and Jeffrey Karpicke's influential 2006 review found that testing yourself on material, actually retrieving it under exam-like conditions, produces far better long-term retention than simply rereading or restudying it, even when the practice test comes with no feedback at all (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006). This is now widely known as the testing effect, and it's counterintuitive precisely because it contradicts how most students prepare: rereading notes feels productive, but retrieval practice, actually generating the answer under pressure, is what the evidence says sticks.

A live viva happening exactly once means students almost never get to apply this. They can reread their notes as much as they like. They rarely get to practice retrieving and articulating an answer out loud, under the same time pressure and cognitive load the real exam will demand, until the day it actually matters.

## What Practice Viva mode changes about that

The Practice Viva mode inside Eduface's oral examination tool lets a lecturer load the actual questions from an upcoming oral exam, and lets students rehearse against those exact questions, out loud, as many times as they want, with a score tracked on each attempt. That's a direct answer to the testing effect gap. Students aren't guessing at what might come up and rereading around it. They're retrieving the specific material, out loud, under conditions close to the real exam, repeatedly, which is exactly the practice Roediger and Karpicke's research says produces durable learning rather than a superficial sense of familiarity.

## Setting it up inside Moodle

Because the tool connects through LTI 1.3, there's no plugin to install and nothing for your IT team to maintain separately. A lecturer configures the session, choosing Practice Viva mode and loading the question set for the upcoming exam, and the session gets pushed to students directly through their existing Moodle course. Students open the link, speak their answers in the browser, and each attempt is scored and logged automatically, so both the student and the lecturer can see the trend across attempts rather than just a single score. The whole thing is typically live within 48 hours of setup, and it's GDPR compliant by design, which matters given oral responses are being recorded.

## What this actually changes in practice

The most common effect providers report isn't a jump in the top grades. It's a narrowing of the gap at the bottom, students who would otherwise freeze under the pressure of a genuinely novel, first-time oral format get enough repetition in beforehand that the format itself stops being the obstacle. That's a meaningful distinction. A viva is meant to test understanding of the subject. Without practice, it partly ends up testing comfort with the format instead, and those two things are not the same skill, even though a single unpracticed attempt conflates them.

There's also a lighter, less measurable benefit worth naming: confidence. Students who've rehearsed the actual format, even without knowing the exact follow-up questions an examiner might ask on the day, walk in having already experienced what it feels like to speak their answer under time pressure. That familiarity alone tends to reduce the performance-anxiety tax that a completely novel format imposes on a first attempt.

## FAQ

**Why are oral exams suddenly being called "AI-proof"?**
Because a live, unscripted conversation about a student's own work is far harder to outsource to AI than a written submission is. That's driving a well documented renewed interest in oral assessment across higher education, though as our companion piece on Canvas covers, "AI-proof" only holds if the exam itself is consistent and well designed, not just oral for its own sake.

**Does Practice Viva mode use the exact questions from the real exam?**
Yes. The lecturer loads the actual question set from the upcoming oral exam, so students are genuinely rehearsing the material they'll be assessed on, not a generic simulation.

**Is there a limit to how many times a student can practice?**
No. Students can run through the practice questions as many times as they find useful, with each attempt scored so they, and their lecturer, can see whether performance is actually improving with repetition.

**Does this replace the graded oral exam itself?**
No. Practice Viva mode is specifically the rehearsal tool. The graded version is a separate mode, covered in our companion piece on running oral exams in Canvas, which applies equally to Moodle.

**Do we need a Moodle plugin to set this up?**
No. The integration runs through LTI 1.3, so there's nothing to install, and a session is typically ready to use within 48 hours.
