Brief summary
UTI instructors mark lab documents by hand. They teach eight to ten hours a day and then spend another two to four hours marking short answer questions, most of it in the evening. Blackboard Ultra's short answer autograding cannot carry this, because an answer that is one character off is counted as wrong. With roughly 1,500 instructors, this is the largest block of time UTI has no way to get back, and it costs UTI people: instructors can earn more in the field and have no marking waiting for them in the evening.
Students mostly get occasional verbal feedback. Nothing of that is written down, so what a student was told stays between that student and that instructor. Nobody above the classroom can see whether feedback was given, whether it holds up, or whether it is the same in Phoenix as it is in Bloomfield on the same curriculum. Written feedback per criterion turns that into something you can read back, compare between campuses and act on, and it gives students something to work from when they prepare for the next lab.
Eduface marks assignments inside Blackboard Ultra against your own answer keys, and writes feedback per criterion instead of only a score. Where a lab has no rubric, the model is instructed on the answer key itself, so it works for short answers, data tables and calculations alike.
The goal for UTI is to partly relieve instructors of their marking burden, and to give students written feedback in Blackboard Ultra where today that feedback is mostly verbal.
Our mutual aim is to go live in Blackboard Ultra with the first programs. This document is the shared plan to get there, and both teams keep it up to date.
