# Sales Handbook — Mastery Plan (v1)

Goal: Dante knows the [Sales & Prospecting Handbook v1](sales-handbook-v1.md) cold — vocabulary, the 6 steps, every gate, and can apply it to a live deal under pressure.

## How we'll learn it (the method, and why)

The research is settled on what actually moves knowledge into long-term memory. Two techniques beat everything else, and they compound when combined:

- **Active recall** — retrieve from memory instead of rereading. One recall test → ~80% retention after a week vs ~34% for rereading. So: closed-book quizzing, not skim-and-nod.
- **Spaced repetition** — review at growing intervals (day 1, day 3, day 7, day 16, day 30+). Beats cramming for durable recall.
- **Scenario role-play** — for a *sales* methodology, application beats memorization. Learners retain ~75% from active simulation vs ~5% from reading. Knowing the gate is step one; deploying it in a live call is the real test. Weekly/bi-weekly practice is the cadence that sticks.

So the plan is layered: lock the facts with recall + spacing, then prove mastery with applied scenarios.

## Four levels of testing (we climb these)

1. **Recall** — vocabulary, the 6 steps in order, what each gate is. Flashcard-style, fast.
2. **Comprehension** — explain *why* a step exists, the difference between coach vs champion, the four EB question categories.
3. **Application** — "Here's a deal situation. What stage is it in? What's missing to pass the gate? What's your next move?" Scenario-based.
4. **Mastery (closed-book mock)** — full timed exam + a live role-play where Dante runs a stage (e.g. an EB meeting or a procurement push-back) with Claude playing the buyer.

## Starter question bank (grows over time)

### Level 1 — Recall
- Define: economic buyer, champion, coach, POV, gates, enemy.
- What's the one-line difference between a coach and a champion?
- List the 6 steps in order.
- Name the 7 basics of sales.
- What are the four categories of questions the EB will ask us?

### Level 2 — Comprehension
- Why is the EB meeting called the "Go/No-Go" stage?
- Why should you ideally meet the EB *before* the POV?
- What are the three written documents produced in Scoping?
- What are the three forms of trust a champion needs to feel?
- Why do we study the institution's annual reports?
- Why must concessions be *traded, never given*?

### Level 3 — Application (scenarios)
- "A contact at a university is asking great questions and clearly cares, but admits they can't get budget reallocated or set criteria. Coach or champion? What do you do next?"
- "You've run a great pilot but the champion only agrees with 80% of the summary report. Have you passed the POV gate? Why/why not?"
- "Procurement is pushing for a 20% discount and offering nothing back. What do you do?"
- "You have discovered pain and identified players but haven't confirmed a buying deadline. Which gate are you stuck at, and what's missing?"

### Level 4 — Mastery (role-play prompts)
- Run a full EB meeting: Claude plays a skeptical Dean, Dante delivers the 9-point EB presentation and lands the Go.
- Defend a business case against a procurement officer trying to reopen scope.
- Cold-test: Claude names a random deal situation, Dante names the stage + gate + next move in under 30 seconds.
