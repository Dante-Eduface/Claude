### 2026-04-30 | meeting | Bath spa x Eduface AI Assessment webinar | id=acti_BsM3o0SF9bf4lKb2EaAgpvH8owsmbojZ2yLXi6GDZDk
Deelnemers: dante.torbed@eduface.me, erika.keizere@sunderland.ac.uk, jeroen.van.gessel@eduface.me
SUMMARY:
- Discussed Eduface's proprietary AI model trained for consistent educational grading and feedback, achieving 96% accuracy at Bath University with 90% greater energy efficiency than commercial models.
- Identified University of Sunderland's key challenges: inconsistent feedback from multiple markers, high workload with 300-400 student intakes per term, and 90% international student population requiring careful language feedback.
- Outlined Eduface features including customizable rubric-based feedback, program-level comment banks, and module leader analytics for tracking marker consistency.
- Presented pilot pricing of £3,500-4,000 upfront plus conditional success fee, with rollout costs ranging £20,000-40,000 per year depending on contract length.
- Confirmed institution is evaluating multiple tools including FeedbackFruits and will present findings to leadership team within several weeks for final decision.
### Institution Background and Context
- University of Sunderland in London is exploring AI assessment tools to support their marking and feedback processes.
- The institution serves over 90% international students, with English not being their first language for the majority.
- The university has four student intake periods per year, with some programs receiving 300-400 student entries.
- An AI policy is currently in development, allowing AI tools for assessment support such as structuring, layout, and grammar checking.
- Program-specific AI policies are being created to accommodate different assessment types across tourism, hospitality, and other disciplines.
### Current Assessment Challenges
- Consistency and standardization in feedback between multiple markers is a significant issue.
- High staff workload due to marking volume creates delays in assessment turnaround and feedback provision.
- Variation in marking and feedback quality between different tutors marking the same cohort affects student experience negatively.
- The institution offers CPD sessions through the Academic Development Centre to address marking consistency issues.
- While some modules have rubrics, they are not universally applied across all programs at the university.
### Use Cases and Implementation Approach
- The institution is willing to explore both formative and summative AI assessment feedback.
- Formative feedback would be provided during the writing process (weeks 2, 4, 6) with summative feedback at assignment completion.
- The university prefers AI as an assistant to generate feedback suggestions rather than as a comparison tool to save time.
- Lecturers would be able to adjust AI-generated feedback and add personal comments as needed.
- The institution is interested in establishing a learning partnership similar to Bath University's implementation with shared results and insights.
### Eduface Tool Features and Technical Specifications
- Eduface has developed its own large language model trained specifically on education and didactic principles of grading and consistent feedback.
- The model contains 120 billion parameters, approximately 10 times smaller than commercial models like ChatGPT and Gemini.
- The proprietary model provides approximately 90% greater energy efficiency compared to commercial large language models.
- Current pilot results with Bath University show 96% accuracy in grading, with the AI model differing by only 4% from human markers.
- The tool achieved 96% accuracy specifically because markers review their own grades first then compare with AI suggestions, creating a comparison study.
### Tool Capabilities and Consistency
- Eduface uses a proprietary model rather than API-based models due to consistency issues with commercial language models.
- The custom model does not produce inconsistent results when processing the same assignment multiple times.
- Lecturers can create personalized banks of standardized comments for frequent feedback, improving grading efficiency.
- Comments and rubrics can be tailored program by program, not just at the university-wide level.
- Module leaders can view analytics showing marking differences between different tutors and be alerted if markers deviate significantly from AI grades.
### Pilot Program Structure and Costs
- Eduface offers a six-month pilot period covering one or two modules with support from a dedicated point of contact.
- Pilot programs require universities to set 2-3 critical success factors, such as 90% grading accuracy or 30% time savings on marking.
- Upfront pilot fees are approximately £3,500 to £4,000.
- An additional success fee of £3,500 to £4,000 applies if critical success factors are achieved.
- The success fee is waived if the institution proceeds to a full rollout agreement.
### Rollout Pricing and Cost Calculations
- Rollout pricing is highly dependent on infrastructure requirements and contract length rather than per-assignment usage.
- For an institution with approximately 5,000-6,000 students, costs typically range from £20,000 per year (multi-year contracts) to £40,000 per year (single-year contracts).
- Pricing is flexible based on GPU reservation periods, with longer-term contracts resulting in lower annual costs.
- Unlike API-based models, Eduface does not charge per token or per assignment processed.
- The cost structure requires advance commitment to reserve GPU infrastructure, making pricing dependent on annual or multi-year selections.
### Sustainability Advantages
- Eduface's proprietary model is approximately 10 times smaller than commercial large language models that contain 1.6-2 trillion parameters.
- The smaller model requires significantly less electricity, cooling, and hardware resources to operate.
- Per-assignment processing in Eduface achieves approximately 90% greater energy efficiency compared to commercial language models.
- Eduface is actively researching further optimization to reduce environmental impact even more.
### Competitive Landscape and Next Steps
- University of Sunderland is currently evaluating multiple assessment tools, including FeedbackFruits, which is identified as a key competitor.
- The institution plans to present options to senior leadership, including the principal lecturer leading academic development and the digital team.
- A decision timeline is planned for several weeks out, pending availability of key decision-makers who are currently on leave.
- The institution may choose to implement either a single tool or multiple tools depending on institutional fit.
