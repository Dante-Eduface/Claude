# MEDDPICC states and gates, coaching reference

Reference for coaching Eduface deals on the internal MEDDPICC grid. It defines
every state a deal element can be in, the proof required to claim that state, and
the gate that must be passed to reach the next one.

Use this to answer questions like "where does this deal really stand", "what is
the next step on Bath Spa", "is my champion a real champion", "why is this deal
stuck". You are a sales coach for the user, not a form filler.

---

## How to coach with this

**Ask for proof, not opinion.** Every state below has a proof line. If the user
cannot state the proof, the deal is not in that state. "I think they like us" is
not proof. "The DVC said in the meeting that this is a priority for the next
budget round" is proof.

**Default to the lower state.** When the evidence sits between two states, the
deal is in the lower one. A grid that flatters the deal puts unqualified deals in
the forecast, which is worse than no grid.

**Watch for happy ears.** The most common errors, in order of damage:
a coach mistaken for a champion, a friendly meeting mistaken for a Go, a generic
ROI slide mistaken for a metric, and a senior title mistaken for the economic
buyer. Challenge all four every time.

**One weak element sets the ceiling.** A deal is only as advanced as its weakest
critical element. Champion and Economic Buyer carry the most weight, so a
brilliant score everywhere else with a weak champion is still a weak deal.

**Always end with one action per weak element.** The single thing that would move
it up one state. That is what the user works on next.

## Part 1: the six sales stages and their gates

The stage is the furthest gate the deal has genuinely passed, not the furthest
conversation that has happened. Do not skip steps.

### 1. Discovery
Investigating the pain, the players and the business gain. Not selling, but
investigating. Discovery is an art: you cannot fire questions without giving
value back, so trade insight, product capability and customer stories in return.

**Gate to leave Discovery.** Confirmed by email to the customer: the discovered
pain, the players involved, why they need to buy now with a buying schedule or
deadline, the desired business outcome, and detailed next steps in the process.
Solution fit verified. This is the point to bring in the founders.

### 2. Scoping
Quantify and implicate the pain found in discovery. Measure the as is process and
define the to be process, giving a preliminary ROI and a cost justification.
Universities live with hundreds of pains that do not threaten funding or
operations, so the job is to prove this one does.

**Gate to leave Scoping.** Three written documents exist: a cost justification
with as is and to be metrics, a POV plan tied to that cost justification, and a
preliminary pricing proposal. Plus a champion who is educated and tested. The
documents must reach the economic buyer, directly or through the champion.

### 3. EB meeting
The Go or No Go stage. Only the economic buyer can confirm whether buying is a
priority. Ideally you meet them before the POV so the buying criteria are framed
around Eduface's differentiators before the evaluation starts.

You present, you do not discover: a summary of findings, the as is state, its
negative consequences, the proposed to be state, its benefits, the capabilities
required to get there (the criteria you want), customer stories with before and
after numbers, your solution against those criteria, and confirmation of the
remaining decision steps.

Expect four categories of question: muchness (what does it cost, what do we
gain), soonness (how fast to implement, how fast to ROI), sureness (will it work
here, can I speak to a reference), easyness (how easy to implement and to learn).

**Gate to leave the EB meeting.** You either got the Go to start the POV with set
criteria, or you did not. The EB confirms they will order after a successful POV,
subject only to legal review and price negotiation.

### 4. Validation event, the POV
Treat a POV like a competition you are wagering money on: know the rules before
you enter. Know the evaluation matrix before it starts, and make sure the criteria
are tailored to what Eduface uniquely does well.

Never start a POV without: a confirmed champion, criteria and scoring matrix
framed with that champion, a cost justification where the gain far exceeds the
pain, and priority, budget, authority, timing and remaining steps verified with
the EB.

During: reconfirm the as is and to be metrics, adjust them with new information,
and capture material evidence of the solution solving the pain. After: a summary
report shared in a physical meeting with the champion, who must agree one hundred
percent with every element, and an updated cost justification shared with the EB.

**Gate to leave the POV.** A summary report plus an updated cost justification,
with material evidence that the solution solved the problem.

### 5. Business case and final proposal
The internal document the institution uses to justify the purchase, written from
their perspective as if they wrote it. Covers their corporate objectives and
strategic initiatives, the business problem, how it blocked those initiatives,
before and after scenarios, a final cost justification in their terms (student
scores, relieved workload, more feedback per teacher), and an implementation plan.

This is the point of maximum buyer risk. Reduce it with reference calls to
customers who solved the same pain, and by reminding them of the cost
justification. A dated implementation plan also creates urgency: start module by
date A, inform faculties by B, grade the first paper by C, hit the result by D, in
order to finalise by E.

**Gate to leave the business case.** A completed case written from the
institution's perspective, validated by the champion as accurate and usable
internally, aligned to top level priorities, containing quantified as is and to be
metrics, a final cost justification based on POV results, and an implementation
plan with dates. The EB has confirmed intent to proceed subject to legal and
procurement.

### 6. Negotiate and close
Procurement is the last hurdle. They rarely question the need for the solution;
their job is to cut price, reduce risk and optimise terms. If the business case,
EB alignment and cost justification are not locked before procurement engages, the
deal gets negotiated down.

Negotiation protects value, it does not win on price. Concessions are approved
internally, documented, and traded, never given. Acceptable trades: discount for
longer contract duration, faster signature, broader deployment scope, or
referenceability. If procurement asks for concessions without offering anything,
escalate to the champion and the EB. If procurement becomes the decision maker,
the deal is already in trouble.

Before entering procurement you must be able to restate: why they are buying,
why now, what happens if they delay, and what value they expect. If you cannot,
you are not ready to negotiate.

**Gate to close.** Signed contract with agreed commercial terms, legal approval
completed, and a confirmed implementation start date.

**Forecast rule.** Only deals with a confirmed business case, EB commitment and an
agreed signature date belong in the forecast. Verbal reassurance from procurement
is not commitment.

## Part 2: the eight elements, state by state

For each element: what each state means, the proof that justifies claiming it, and
the gate to the next state.

### METRICS
The quantified value the customer gains. Customers do not buy on price, they buy
on value. Without numbers there is no cost justification and no business case, so
the deal stalls at buyer risk.

| State | What it means | Proof required |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| UNKNOWN | No value or success metrics defined | Nothing measured |
| CURRENT STATE | The as is situation is measured | Real numbers from the customer's own data: volumes, hours, cost, turnaround, satisfaction scores |
| TARGET STATE | A cost case exists and the gain clearly exceeds the cost | A written cost justification with as is and to be figures, confirmed by the champion |
| POV VALIDATED | The metrics were proven in the pilot | Material evidence captured during the POV, not projections |
| EB AGREED | The economic buyer accepts the final cost justification | The EB has seen the updated numbers and did not dispute them |
| INSTITUTION OWNED | The institution states the value in its own terms | The business case uses their language and their numbers |

**Gates.**
- To CURRENT STATE: get their actual figures. Volume of submissions per year, students, courses and teachers affected, minutes per submission, who does the marking and at what cost, turnaround days, current feedback scores and complaints, rework such as remarks and appeals, and what they already spend on tools or hired capacity.
- To TARGET STATE: agree the expected time per submission, the expected turnaround, the exact scope for year one, the improvement on the metric leadership steers on, and what the freed teacher time gets reinvested in. Then set it against licence plus implementation cost. Rule of thumb: below roughly three times the cost it is not a compelling case, so stay at CURRENT STATE.
- To POV VALIDATED: run the pilot and capture evidence against those same figures.
- To EB AGREED: put the updated cost justification in front of the EB.
- To INSTITUTION OWNED: have the champion write it into their internal case.

**Trap.** A generic ROI slide is not a metric. If the number did not come from the customer's own data, it does not count above CURRENT STATE.

### ECONOMIC BUYER
The person with discretionary use of the funds who can reallocate budget and make
the final call. Only they confirm whether this is a priority.

| State | What it means | Proof required |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| UNKNOWN | Not identified | No name |
| IDENTIFIED | We know who it is, no access | A name and role, confirmed by the champion as the budget holder |
| PROFILED | We understand their priorities | Their objectives and personal measures, ideally from annual reports and the champion |
| MET | The meeting happened, value framed to their criteria | A meeting took place in which we presented as is, to be, and criteria |
| GO OBTAINED | They confirm priority and signal budget | They said the problem ranks high and they would fund it if the POV proves the case |
| SPONSORING | They actively protect the deal | They intervene when procurement pushes too far |

**Gates.**
- To IDENTIFIED: ask the champion who can reallocate budget and sign alone.
- To PROFILED: study the annual report and ask the champion what this person is measured on.
- To MET: the champion opens the door, or you present jointly with the champion.
- To GO OBTAINED: ask directly. Where does this rank on your priority list? Which business measure does it affect most? Would you allocate budget if the POV proves X? Besides you, who else approves a purchase this size? What are the remaining steps after a successful POV?
- To SPONSORING: keep them informed through procurement so they defend the value.

**Trap.** A senior contact is not automatically the EB. Test: can this person reallocate budget and decide alone? If someone else must sign, they are not the EB.

### CHAMPION
An internal advocate with both authority and influence who wins personally if
Eduface wins. Coaches get POVs, champions get large deals. This is where happy
ears do the most damage.

| State | What it means | Proof required |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| NONE | No advocate | Nobody is helping |
| COACH | Gives information, no influence | They answer questions and arrange meetings, but cannot shape decisions |
| CHAMPION FOUND | Authority and influence, plus a personal win | They sit meaningfully in the org chart, ask the most probing questions, and you can name what they personally gain |
| EDUCATED | They sell our differentiators internally | They can explain what sets Eduface apart and why it is the only tool that solves the problem |
| TESTED | Proven under the test below | They pass the champion test and actively move the deal |
| EB ACCESS | They open the EB meeting and shape criteria | They set up the meeting with the economic buyer and influence the buying criteria |

**The champion test.** Only test after you have earned trust and shown you are
solving a major institutional issue. A true champion can explain how purchasing
decisions are made here, describe the typical evaluation process, name who was
involved in the last major purchase, walk you through the current as is process,
explain why this pain is a major pain and what happens if it is not addressed, and
describe how competitors delivered value in meetings with them.

Beyond answering, they act: introducing other stakeholders, influencing the buying
criteria, sharing evaluation metrics, connecting you to legal and procurement, and
setting up the EB meeting. Someone who answers well but does none of the doing is
a COACH.

**Gates.**
- To CHAMPION FOUND: identify their personal win. Align to a problem that affects the most important measures of the institution's highest authority, so solving it gives them visibility and standing.
- To EDUCATED: teach them the differentiators, the buying criteria we want, and rehearse. You can role play the EB meeting with them.
- To TESTED: run the test above.
- To EB ACCESS: ask them to set up the EB meeting.

**Trap.** Seniority is not influence, and friendliness is not advocacy. True champions have business acumen, avoid small pains, and will not be seen with unprepared salespeople because they put their reputation on the line. Earn that through homework on their institution.

### IDENTIFY PAIN
The pain, its root cause and the quantified consequence of doing nothing. No pain,
no deal.

| State | What it means | Proof required |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| UNKNOWN | No pain identified | Nothing found |
| DISCOVERED | Pain and root cause found | They described what is happening, why it exists, and what changed |
| IMPLICATED | The cost of doing nothing is quantified | A number attached to the consequence |
| COMPELLING EVENT | Tied to a deadline or cycle | A named date: term start, enrollment, budget cycle, accreditation review, survey publication |
| EB OWNS | The EB agrees it is a priority | The EB said so |
| INSTITUTIONAL | Linked to top level measures | Tied to enrollment, retention, financial health or risk in the business case |

**Gates.**
- To DISCOVERED: ask what makes this a problem now, why it exists (process, systems or resourcing), what changed recently, and whether it is one faculty or the whole institution.
- To IMPLICATED: ask how often it happens, how many students and staff are affected, whether it is getting worse, and what it costs in time and money.
- To COMPELLING EVENT: ask why now rather than next academic year, and what happens if it is not resolved before the next term or enrollment cycle.
- To EB OWNS: confirm it in the EB meeting.
- To INSTITUTIONAL: link it to retention, enrollment stability, student success funding, or reputational risk.

**Trap.** A pain nobody is funded to fix is a small pain. If leadership has not linked it to money or risk, it is DISCOVERED, not IMPLICATED. Remember that doing nothing is the default winner without a compelling event.

### DECISION CRITERIA
What the customer will judge solutions on. Shape these early toward Eduface's
differentiators, because if the criteria favour a competitor you lose before the
evaluation starts.

| State | What it means | Proof required |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| UNKNOWN | Criteria not known | No idea what they will judge on |
| SURFACED | Some criteria heard | Mentioned in discovery, unconfirmed |
| SET WITH CHAMPION | Shaped together with the champion | The champion confirms the list and helped build it |
| FRAMED TO EB | Presented as required capabilities at the EB meeting | The EB heard our differentiators framed as the capabilities needed for the to be state |
| MATRIX WON | We score highest in the POV evaluation matrix | The scored matrix exists and we lead it |
| LOCKED | Fixed and not reopened | Procurement is not renegotiating scope or criteria |

**Gates.**
- To SURFACED: ask what criteria will matter most when evaluating a solution like this.
- To SET WITH CHAMPION: build the list with them, including security, GDPR and DPA, LMS fit for Canvas, Brightspace, Moodle or Blackboard, accuracy and accessibility.
- To FRAMED TO EB: present the required capabilities in the EB meeting, before the POV.
- To MATRIX WON: agree the scoring matrix before the pilot starts, then win it.
- To LOCKED: keep the business case and criteria closed before procurement engages.

**Trap.** Assuming you know the criteria. Until the champion confirms them you are guessing, which is SURFACED at best.

### DECISION PROCESS
The steps, people and timeline between here and a signature.

| State | What it means | Proof required |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| UNKNOWN | Not known | No idea how they buy |
| OUTLINED | Broad steps and people known | A rough sequence |
| MAPPED | Full process and approvers mapped | Every step and every approver can be named |
| EB CONFIRMED | The EB confirmed the remaining steps | Confirmed in the EB meeting after the Go |
| CLOSE PLAN | Mutual plan with dates and owners | A written plan both sides agreed |
| ON TRACK | Signature date set, approvers aligned | A date exists and nothing is blocking |

**Gates.**
- To OUTLINED: ask who else needs to be convinced and what would cause this to stall or be deprioritised.
- To MAPPED: have the champion walk the full process, including who was involved in the last major purchase.
- To EB CONFIRMED: ask the EB directly what the remaining steps are after a successful POV.
- To CLOSE PLAN: write the implementation plan backwards from the required go live date so the signature date follows from it.
- To ON TRACK: confirm every approver is aligned.

**Trap.** "They said a few weeks" is not a process. Score MAPPED only when each step and approver can be named.

### PAPER PROCESS
Procurement, legal, security, data processing and the signature path. For Eduface
this includes the DPA, security review, and the Jisc or HEAnet framework route.
These add weeks and are the classic reason a won deal slips a quarter.

| State | What it means | Proof required |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| UNKNOWN | Not known | No idea what the paper path is |
| IDENTIFIED | The route is identified | We know whether it runs via a framework, and who owns legal and procurement |
| MAPPED | Steps, owners and lead times mapped | Named owners and realistic durations |
| STARTED | Reviews under way | Legal, security and DPA review actually started |
| ADVANCING | Progressing with value protected | Concessions traded rather than given, no blockers |
| CLEARED | Ready to sign | Legal approved |

**Gates.**
- To IDENTIFIED: ask early about governance, IT and data privacy constraints, and whether they buy through a framework.
- To MAPPED: get lead times, because these determine whether the close date is realistic.
- To STARTED: send the DPA and security documentation before the POV ends, not after.
- To ADVANCING: trade every concession. Approve internally, document it, and get something of equal or greater value in return.
- To CLEARED: legal sign off.

**Trap.** Leaving paper untouched until late. If the deal is at POV or beyond while paper is still UNKNOWN, a gate was skipped and the close date is fiction.

### COMPETITION
The enemy, defined broadly: FeedbackFruits, homegrown or in house tools, and the
do nothing option. Also the competitor's champion inside the account.

| State | What it means | Proof required |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| UNKNOWN | Landscape not known | We do not know who else is in play |
| IDENTIFIED | Competitors and enemy champion known | We know who they are evaluating and who inside supports them |
| POSITIONED | Our differentiators set against theirs | A clear articulation of what only Eduface does |
| PREFERRED | We are leading | The champion says we are the frontrunner |
| TRAPS SET | Criteria favour us, objections handled | The criteria include capabilities competitors lack |
| LOCKED | They commit over alternatives | Stated commitment to us |

**Gates.**
- To IDENTIFIED: ask what they have tried already, what worked partially and where it broke down, and who else they are speaking to.
- To POSITIONED: articulate the differentiators against the named alternatives, including doing nothing.
- To PREFERRED: get the champion to confirm where we stand.
- To TRAPS SET: write the differentiators into the decision criteria before the POV.
- To LOCKED: get commitment before procurement.

**Trap.** Forgetting that doing nothing is the most common competitor. Without a compelling event it usually wins, however good the product.

## Part 3: skipped steps

A skipped step is when a deal has advanced past a gate that one element never
actually cleared. The grid flags any element sitting two or more states behind the
declared sales stage.

Typical examples worth calling out:

- **Stage POV, Champion at COACH.** A pilot is running with an untested champion. Nobody is selling internally when we are not in the room, and nobody guarantees the POV summary will be accepted.
- **Stage POV, Economic buyer at IDENTIFIED.** No Go was ever obtained. The pilot may prove everything and still hit a wall, because nobody confirmed it is a funded priority.
- **Stage business case, Paper process at UNKNOWN.** The close date is fiction until procurement and legal lead times are known.
- **Stage EB meeting, Metrics at UNKNOWN.** There is nothing to present. The EB meeting needs as is figures, consequences and a to be state.

What to do: do not advance the deal. Requalify the lagging element and make it the
top priority action for that deal. A skipped step is the most valuable output of
the grid, so name it plainly rather than smoothing it over.

## Part 4: reading the overall score

The percentage measures how much of the deal has been genuinely qualified and
evidenced. It is not a guess at close probability. It only means something next to
the stage the deal claims to be in.

A deal that is on track scores roughly:

| Stage | On track |
|-------|----------|
| Discovery | about 17% |
| Scoping | about 37% |
| EB Go obtained | about 56% |
| POV won | about 67% |
| Business case | about 78% |
| Negotiate and close | 85% and up |

Below the benchmark for the declared stage means a gate was skipped somewhere, so
requalify before advancing. Well above it means either a genuinely strong deal or
an overscored element, so check the highest claims for proof.

## Part 5: discovery question bank

Use these to gather the evidence a state requires.

**Problem and root cause.** What makes this a problem for your institution today?
Why does this issue exist, process, systems or resourcing? What changed recently
that made it more visible? Is it isolated to one faculty or consistent across the
university?

**Timing and urgency.** Why address this now rather than next academic year? What
happens if it is not resolved before the next enrollment or term cycle? When did
leadership first recognise it? Is there a deadline driving this, a budget cycle,
accreditation review or survey result?

**Frequency and scale.** How often does this occur in a typical term? How many
students or staff are affected each time? Is the impact increasing or flat?

**Prior attempts.** What have you tried already? What worked partially and where
did it break down? What prevented progress? Was it handled locally or centrally?

**Metrics and impact.** Which institutional metrics are most impacted? How are you
measuring this today? What feedback are you hearing from students and faculty?

**Ownership and influence.** Who is ultimately accountable for improving this
outcome? Who feels the pain day to day? Who would need to sign off on a change?

**Organizational impact.** How is this affecting staff capacity or morale? Where
are people compensating manually? What trade offs are teams making?

**Dependencies and constraints.** What other systems does this depend on? Where do
handoffs break down? Are there governance, IT or data privacy constraints to know
early?

**Desired outcome.** If this worked exactly as you wanted, what would be different
for students, for staff, for leadership? How would you define success six to
twelve months after fixing it?

**Staff efficiency.** How much staff time goes to repetitive or preventable work?
Which roles are most overloaded and why? If staff had back ten to twenty percent
of their time, where would it be reinvested?

**Change readiness.** What would prevent adoption across the university? Do new
platforms roll out pilot first or full deployment? Who would champion this
internally if it delivers value?

**Financial and strategic alignment.** Is this tied to retention, enrollment
stability or student success funding? Has leadership linked it to financial or
reputational risk? If it paid for itself, how would that be evaluated?

**Decision process.** What criteria will matter most when evaluating a solution
like this? Who else needs to be convinced? What would cause this initiative to
stall or be deprioritised?
