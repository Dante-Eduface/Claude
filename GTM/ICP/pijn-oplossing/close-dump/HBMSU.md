### 2026-08-05 | meeting | Ahmed ElMassry and Dante Torbed | id=acti_EWhxV6nyl8lUkCvVEwtY662CDj8QcLsTXVfImzF8sxH
Deelnemers: a.aziz@hbmsu.ac.ae, a.elmassry@hbmsu.ac.ae, dante.torbed@eduface.me
SUMMARY:
- Discussed AI-based essay grading solution for HBMSU's English 101 course with approximately 500-1,000 learners expected in fall semester.
- Eduface's Moodle-integrated platform uses custom-trained LLM for academic grading with initial setup requiring instructor training (approximately one hour) followed by automated instant feedback.
- Pilot planned with mock exams in summer and first final exam on August 20th, focusing on essay questions within quizzes rather than assignments.
- HBMSU requires consistent grading without human intervention as end goal, though initial rollout may include faculty review until system reliability is verified.
- Next meeting scheduled for Friday, August 11th at 11:30 AM HBMSU time (9:30 AM Eduface time) to demonstrate Moodle integration with essay grading demo.
### HBMSU Overview and Project Context
- Ahmed ElMasry is the general education program coordinator managing 12 courses including English and Psychology across multiple sections.
- HBMSU is the first smart university in the UAE, based in Dubai, with focus on AI-based solutions to automate instructor workload.
- General education courses cover English, Arabic, UAE studies, statistics, math, programming, AI, innovation and entrepreneurship.
- English 101 course is currently live with digitized, self-paced content published in Moodle with mock and final exams ready.
- University aims to transition from instructor-led to self-paced learning with full automation and minimal instructor intervention.
### Specific English 101 Pilot Requirements
- Pilot focuses exclusively on essay grading within quiz questions (150-200 word writing prompts), not assignment submissions.
- Mock exams allow three learner attempts; final exams occur with first test on August 20th and second on August 27th.
- Fall semester implementation expected to serve 500-1,000 learners in English 101 with potential expansion to other general education courses.
- Grading scale within quizzes varies by settings, potentially 1-5 or 1-10 point scales, not pass/fail designation.
- Initial pilot phase will grade mock exams without faculty intervention, while final exams may require faculty review until system reliability is confirmed.
### Eduface Platform and Integration Details
- Eduface built custom large language model trained on academic data after determining general LLMs lacked sufficient consistency and quality.
- Platform is fully integrated with Moodle using LTI 1.3 integration standard for seamless quiz grading within the learning management system.
- Grading occurs directly in Moodle quiz interface; grades are then automatically pushed to Banner student information system via existing automation.
- System provides both numerical grades and qualitative feedback with grading applied at each rubric point, then aggregated into final grade.
- Initial instructor training typically requires approximately one hour to configure the system with specific rubrics and grading expectations.
### Pilot Timeline and Implementation Process
- Dante Torbed confirmed Eduface can be up and running within one week once Moodle integration is complete and assessor is available for instruction.
- Moodle integration typically requires one to two days for technical implementation, with potential security considerations like firewall configurations.
- Legal documentation (GDPR compliant) must be processed through HBMSU procurement before pilot can begin.
- Pricing based on number of courses and support level required; pilot phase includes only support and AI processing costs at reduced rate pending clear requirements documentation.
- Implementation plan with specific dates will be sent by Eduface, with all timeline dates requiring discussion and confirmation from both parties.
### Academic Requirements and Expectations
- System must provide grading consistency across student assessments to ensure fairness and standardization across large enrollments.
- **University aims to reach AI-native university status with full AI-based grading without human review as ultimate goal.**
- One faculty member from English department will be assigned as subject matter expert (SME) to guide system configuration and validation.
- Dean initially prefers faculty verification of grading scores during first pilot run before full automation, though long-term goal is complete AI autonomy.
- Feedback format should include specific guidance such as areas of strength, areas for improvement, and actionable recommendations for students.
### Technical Infrastructure and Considerations
- Faisal Ijaz serves as senior software engineer managing LMS infrastructure; Ali Aziz is learning systems administrator providing technical support.
- University uses Moodle as learning management platform with Banner as student information system for grade recording and reporting.
- Arabic language support for UAE studies course is not currently supported by Eduface platform; supporting Arabic would require significant development effort.
- Quiz structure includes multiple choice questions, short answer questions, and essay components; Eduface solution targets essay grading component specifically.
- Separate adaptive learning project running concurrently allows system to provide remedial content based on learner performance, independent of this grading initiative.
### Demonstration and Follow-up Meeting
- Demo scheduled for Friday, August 11th at 11:30 AM HBMSU time / 9:30 AM Eduface time to showcase Moodle-integrated essay grading.
- Dante will demonstrate system within actual Moodle environment with quiz essays rather than assignment submissions to match HBMSU's specific use case.
- Ahmed requested demo include rubric building process, learner submission workflow, grading logic, feedback format, and grade visibility to students.
- Eduface will provide implementation plan document after demo meeting to clarify all requirements, success criteria, timelines and project scope.
- Meeting invites will be sent to attendees; documents and technical specifications to be shared following the Friday demonstration meeting.
### Project Scope and Related Initiatives
- Currently no AI-assisted assignment grading tools exist within HBMSU's existing systems; this initiative addresses specific need for quiz essay grading.
- Previous agentic AI project generated learning outcomes and content from course syllabi, which faculty members then refined and published to Moodle.
- **HBMSU emphasizes that challenging requirements strengthen vendors' products; previous vendor (Rize) stated HBMSU requirements improved their platform capabilities.**
- Pilot will include success criteria and evaluation process to determine whether to roll out tool across additional courses post-pilot.
- Future expansion potential includes heavy project courses and other general education offerings beyond initial English 101 focus.
### Preferred Workflow and Human-in-the-Loop Considerations
- Ideal workflow: students submit essays through Moodle quiz, AI grades instantly, and grade appears immediately without manual intervention for mock exams.
- For final exams, faculty can review AI-assigned grades and easily adjust if needed by clicking approve or modifying the numerical score.
- University seeks balance between automation and quality assurance, with mock exams fully automated and final exams subject to initial faculty review.
- Long-term goal eliminates manual review entirely once system demonstrates consistent accuracy comparable to faculty grading standards.
- Pilot success depends on achieving high accuracy (example: 89% mark accuracy where AI matches faculty assigned grades) before full automation approval.
