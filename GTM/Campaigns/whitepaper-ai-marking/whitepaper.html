The two outcomesEduface

Two outcomes, from 20+ implementations.

Institutions bring in AI marking and feedback for many reasons. The results we can measure come down to two: consistency and operational efficiency.

Consistency pages 3 to 4

Students are judged on their work, not on who marked it.

Definition

Every paper is marked against the same criteria in the same way: the same mark, the same focus and the same style of feedback, whoever marks it.

9/20

essays ranked both best and worst by experienced examiners

Operational efficiency pages 5 to 6

Lecturer time moves from typing feedback to teaching.

Definition

Less staff time per assignment for the same or better feedback, while the lecturer still approves every comment and every mark.

−85%

time spent typing feedback, up to 20 minutes per assignment

Next stepEduface

Start small. Then roll out what works.

Jisc's advice to institutions starting with AI in assessment: begin small, with a pilot. It matches what we have learned across 20+ implementations.

Phase 1A small group

A few lecturers, one module, one use case. The model is tuned to their rubric.

Phase 2Proven use cases

What worked moves to more modules and more markers.

Phase 3Gradual roll-out

The next use cases are added one at a time, when the institution is ready.

Where institutions often start

The consistency check on a module that is already marked Beta
