# How to Grade Assignments in Blackboard Ultra (Faster): Gradebook, Annotations, Audio Feedback and Reusable Comments

*The click path from the Gradebook to a posted grade, how Bb Annotate, rubrics and audio feedback fit together, what Ultra does and does not offer for reusing comments, and a ranked list of the time-savers that are actually documented.*

By Eduface · October 2026 · 31 min read · Written for instructors, instructional designers and program directors at private and for-profit colleges that run Blackboard Ultra

It is Monday morning and 60 submissions are waiting in your Ultra course. The Gradebook shows a number next to the assignment, and you know from last term that the number is the easy part. Last time you opened each file, typed the same sentence about thesis statements nine times, and posted grades after midnight. This time you want to know which of Ultra's grading tools save real time and which only look like they do.

> **How to grade assignments in Blackboard Ultra**
> Open the course Gradebook, select the Needs Grading icon under the assignment, choose a student on the Submissions page, and grade in Flexible Grading: annotate the file with Bb Annotate, score with the attached rubric, add feedback in the feedback panel, then post the grade. To grade faster, filter to Needs Grading, store repeated comments in the Content Library, record short audio feedback instead of typing, and post all grades once at the end.

> **Key takeaways**
> 1. Start from the Gradebook and the Needs Grading icon, not from the course content page. The icon opens the submissions that are waiting and nothing else.
> 2. Grade with a rubric attached. You select one performance level per criterion and Ultra calculates the total.
> 3. Ultra has one documented comment bank, the Content Library inside Bb Annotate. We found nothing comparable for the overall feedback box or the rubric criterion boxes.
> 4. Audio and video feedback is capped at 5 minutes without a Video Studio license and 20 minutes with one.
> 5. Students see nothing until you post. Assignments do not post automatically unless you turn that setting on.

- **Where to start and the step-by-step path.** Several ways into the submissions, and the order of clicks on the grading page.

- **Posting and problems.** Why students cannot see grades, late submissions and overrides.
- **Where AI fits.** What Blackboard's own AI feedback tools do, and what Eduface adds for the same assignment.

## Where do you start grading in Blackboard Ultra?

Start in the course Gradebook: the Needs Grading icon under each assignment opens the submissions that are waiting. Anthology's help lists several routes in, and which one you use depends on whether you think in courses, assignments or students.¹

Across all your courses, an alert in the Activity Stream, "New submissions ready to grade," takes you to that course's Gradebook. Selecting Grades in the left-hand navigation lists your grading tasks by course, including assignments that are overdue for one or more students.¹ ² Inside a course, the Gradebook opens in grid view, with students on the left and one column per gradable item. A toggle switches to list view, which shows every gradable item with its due date and grading status.³

The To Do panel in the Gradebook sorts your work into three piles: needs grading (oldest first), needs posting, and needs reconciliation, which appears only if the course uses delegated or parallel grading.² Working through "oldest first" is a decent default, because the longest-waiting student is also the one most likely to email you.

The Submissions page shows each student's status, and the statuses matter more than they look. A student with Draft Saved has started but not submitted, and the grading status reads "Nothing to grade." A student photo with a red circle means a late submission or a passed due date with no attempt.¹ If a student had trouble submitting, you can open the ellipsis menu on the Submissions page, select Submit draft attempt, and enter a reason. The reason and your name then appear on the submission receipt.¹

## How do you grade one assignment in Ultra, step by step?

Open the student's submission from the Submissions page, mark it up, score it, add feedback, and move to the next student; Ultra saves rubric scores and feedback as you go.⁴ ⁶ The steps below follow the Flexible Grading page, with the labels Anthology uses.

1. **Open the Gradebook and the item.** From Courses, open the course and select Gradebook at the top. Select the Needs Grading icon under the assignment's column header, or select the assignment name to open the Assessment Details panel and then View submissions.³
2. **Filter to what is left.** On the Submissions page, set the Grading Status filter to Needs Grading, then select a student's name to open the grading page. Expand the Students panel to see who has submitted, or collapse it to give the submission more room.⁴ If you enabled anonymous grading, all names are masked.⁴
3. **Read and mark up the file.** Supported file types open in the browser, and the Bb Annotate options appear on the menu bar for inline comments.⁴ ⁵
4. **Score it.** Select the rubric icon next to the grade pill to open the stacked-view rubric in the side panel, or the expand icon to open the grid view in a separate, movable window. Select a score pill for each criterion.⁶ If there is no rubric, type a grade into the grade pill.
5. **Add feedback.** Expand the feedback panel and type overall feedback, or add an audio or video recording. Under Grader Notes you can leave private notes that students cannot see.⁴
6. **Move on.** Select the next name in the Students panel. For a multiple-attempt assignment, pick the attempt to grade first.⁴
7. **Post.** Use Post Grades to release grades for all graded students, or the menu next to a single grade to post just that one.⁴

You can also skip the grading page for simple scores. In the grid view, hover under the assessment icon until a purple pen icon appears, select it, type grades straight into the text boxes, then select the purple checkmark. The total points are not editable here.⁴ ³

> **Warning: no undo in column editing**
> When you edit a whole column from the grid, Anthology's help states that there is no confirmation dialog for score changes and no undo.³ Use it for complete-or-incomplete work and quick point entries, not for graded essays where you will want a rubric trail.

> **Check on your Blackboard**
> Some options in this guide depend on your institution's settings: anonymous comments in Bb Annotate, Reveal student identity, and every AI feature are described by Anthology as available only if your institution turned them on.⁵ ¹⁵ ⁹ If a button is missing, ask your Blackboard administrator before assuming you did something wrong.

## How do you annotate student work with Bb Annotate?

Bb Annotate is the inline grading tool: it opens the student's file in the browser and lets you comment, highlight, draw and stamp directly on the page.⁵ It handles Word, PowerPoint, Excel, OpenOffice files, PDF, RTF, TXT, common image formats and source code, among others. Macros in Office files are not supported.⁵

Two limits catch people the first week. Submissions created in the assignment editor (typed straight into Blackboard) are not compatible with inline grading, and annotation sessions expire after one hour, with a warning before they do. Saved annotations, feedback and files stay on the page, so you resume where you stopped.⁵

The toolbar covers more than most instructors use. Comment puts your note in a panel next to the submission. The Highlighter lets you highlight, strike through, underline, squiggle or comment on selected text. Shapes, freehand Drawing and the Text tool handle everything else, and your selection for each tool is saved between submissions, so you set your preferred color and font once.⁵ Students can open the annotated file afterwards but cannot add their own annotations.⁵

One known issue is worth passing on to students and co-graders. Some browsers' built-in PDF viewer may not display every annotation, so Anthology recommends opening annotated PDFs in a native viewer such as Adobe Acrobat.⁵

[CARDS: 3]
- **Content Library** | A bank of reusable comments inside Bb Annotate. Add, edit, delete and search comments, then place one on the page with one click. Anthology notes it is only available in SaaS environments.⁵
- **Stamps** | Choose a preloaded stamp or create your own image or stamp. Good for a recurring mark such as "cite the source" or "see rubric, criterion 3".⁵
- **Highlighter with comment** | Highlight a passage and add a comment on the highlight, so the note is anchored to the exact words rather than floating in the margin.⁵
[/CARDS]

## What types of feedback can you give, and when should you use each?

Ultra gives you five places to put feedback: comments on the file, a text box per rubric criterion, an overall feedback box, audio or video, and private grader notes. They differ in what students see and in how much they cost you in time, so decide which job each one does before you start.

| Feedback type | Where you add it | What students see | Limit or note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comments on the file | Bb Annotate on the submission | The annotated file, read-only | Sessions expire after one hour⁵ |
| Criterion feedback | Text box inside each rubric criterion | The rubric, with your selections highlighted | No character limit; plain-text editor⁶ |
| Overall feedback | Feedback panel next to the submission | Your text, after you post | No character limit; shows for every attempt, even ungraded ones⁶ ¹¹ |
| Audio or video | Audio/Video button in the feedback panel | A recording beside your text | 5 minutes, or 20 with a Video Studio license⁷ |
| Grader Notes | Grader Notes in the feedback panel | Nothing: private | Useful as a record for a grade decision⁴ |

*Table 1: Five feedback channels in Ultra. Only the first four are visible to students.*

Yes → green box "Bb Annotate comment or Content Library comment". No → Q2 "Is it about a criterion every student is scored on?" Yes → blue box "Rubric criterion text box, only when the level descriptor does not already say it". No → Q3 "Does it need tone, nuance or more than about 60 seconds of explanation?" Yes → amber box "Audio or video, 5 minutes maximum without a Video Studio license". No → grey box "Overall feedback: two or three sentences, one next step". A side box on the right reads "Record why you changed a grade: Grader Notes (students cannot see them)".]
*Figure 3: Match the channel to the comment: passage-level notes belong on the file, patterns belong in the rubric, and nuance is quicker spoken than typed.*

Audio and video feedback is the one that most often saves time. In the feedback panel, select Audio/Video, choose whether to record your camera, audio, screen, audio and screen, or camera and screen, and select the Start recording button. When you finish, give the recording a title and description, choose View Only or View and Download for students, preview it, and select Save Video.⁷ Without a Video Studio license, recordings are limited to 5 minutes. With one, the limit is 20 minutes. Anthology also states that recordings made this way do not count against your institution's Blackboard storage entitlement, and that embedded media is not supported in all browsers: Chrome or Firefox gives the best experience.⁴ ⁷

> **Copy-ready: a 60-second audio feedback structure**
> "Hi [name], here is where your paper landed. One: the strongest part was [specific section], because [reason in one sentence]. Two: the biggest gain is available in [criterion], where [specific problem, point to the page]. Three: for the next assignment, try [one concrete change]. Your detailed scoring is in the rubric. Come see me in office hours if you want to talk it through."

## Can you reuse comments in Blackboard Ultra?

Partly: Ultra has one documented comment bank, the Content Library inside Bb Annotate, and we found no saved-comment feature for the overall feedback box or the rubric criterion boxes in Anthology's help for instructors.⁵ ⁶ That is a gap, and the workarounds below are workarounds, not Blackboard features.

[CARDS: 3]
- **Content Library (native)** | Lives in the Bb Annotate menu. Select the plus sign to add a comment, then use Place comment, Copy to Clipboard, Edit or Delete, and search by keyword or phrase. It saves you retyping on the file, and Copy to Clipboard gives you the text to paste elsewhere.⁵
- **Rubric level descriptions (native)** | The Show descriptions toggle displays each performance level's description, and students can expand criteria to see theirs after you post. Writing a sharp descriptor once replaces a typed comment on every paper.⁶
- **A snippet file of your own (tip, not a Blackboard feature)** | Keep one document with 10 to 15 labeled comments and paste from it. A text-expander app on your computer does the same with a short code. Blackboard offers nothing for this in the feedback box.
[/CARDS]

The Content Library is where the time is. Anthology describes it as a bank of reusable comments: you add a comment with the plus sign, edit or delete it later, and search by keyword or phrase.⁵ If you grade the same assignment type each term, spend 15 minutes before you start loading the comments you expect to write most.

> **Tip: a starter comment library for a written business assignment**
> Load these as separate Content Library comments, adapt the wording, and add your own after the first ten papers.
> 1. "Your thesis is stated, but the paper does not yet argue it. Name the claim in one sentence and test every paragraph against it."
> 2. "Evidence needs a source. Add the author and year, or remove the claim."
> 3. "This describes the framework. Show how it applies to the case facts."
> 4. "Strong point. Develop it with a second example."
> 5. "This paragraph has two ideas. Split it, and give each its own topic sentence."
> 6. "The conclusion repeats the introduction. Say what the reader should now do or believe."
> 7. "Check the citation format against the syllabus. [style]"
> 8. "Unclear. Rewrite this sentence so the subject and the action are obvious."

Two cautions. First, Anthology notes that the Content Library is only available in SaaS environments, so a self-hosted or managed-hosting Blackboard may not have it.⁵ Second, reusing a comment does not make it right for every paper. A comment that does not fit the passage it sits on reads as a form letter and costs you more trust than it saves in time.

Blackboard also has AI tools in this area, and they come with a licensing caveat. Anthology's AVA assistant includes a Summarize option that drafts overall feedback from a rubric you already completed, and a Rewrite option that polishes your own notes once you have typed at least 30 characters. Both show an AI-generated banner and let you accept, reject or regenerate.⁸ Anthology's administrator help says AVA features were included in the Blackboard license until June 30, 2026, and that a specific AVA license is required after that date.⁹ Anthology's July 2025 announcement describes the same period as a free trial.¹⁰ We could not find a price or confirm what your institution licenses, so ask your administrator before you plan around either button. The second post in this series covers [how to build the rubric these tools depend on](/resources/blog/how-to-create-and-use-a-rubric-in-blackboard-ultra), and the third compares [what Blackboard's AI does and does not do](/resources/blog/blackboard-ultra-ai-grading).

## How do you grade faster in Blackboard Ultra?

Grade faster by cutting the work around the reading, because the reading itself does not shrink: start from Needs Grading, grade with a rubric, load a comment bank, speak long feedback instead of typing it, and post all grades once. The table ranks ten time-savers by how much they usually save and ties each to the Anthology page that documents it.

| Rank | Time-saver | Why it saves time | Where it is documented |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grade with a rubric attached | One click per criterion; the total calculates itself; criterion feedback saves automatically | Grade with Rubrics⁶ |
| 2 | Needs Grading filter and To Do panel | You never open a student who is already done | My Grading Action Items; Flexible Grading² ⁴ |
| 3 | Content Library in Bb Annotate | Retype nothing you wrote before; tool choices persist | Bb Annotate⁵ |
| 4 | Audio or video overall feedback | Speaking is faster than typing for long, nuanced comments | Record Audio and Video⁷ |
| 5 | Keyboard use in the rubric | Tab between criteria and use the up and down arrows to select a level | Grade with Rubrics⁶ |
| 6 | Post all grades once | One confirmation instead of one post per student | Gradebook grid view; Post Grades³ ¹⁸ |
| 7 | Grade a test by question | All students' answers to one essay question on one page | Flexible Grading⁴ |
| 8 | Delegated grading | Split a large class among TAs or graders | Delegated Grading²⁴ |
| 9 | AVA Summarize and Rewrite | Drafts overall feedback from your rubric selections; needs the AVA license | AVA - AI Virtual Assistant⁸ ⁹ |
| 10 | Upload gradebook (CSV) | Enter grades offline, with caveats about overrides | Upload a File to Gradebook²⁵ |

*Table 2: Ten documented time-savers, ranked. The ranking is ours, from how many steps each removes per submission; the documentation is Anthology's.*

Each of the lower rows has a catch. Delegated grading supports individual submissions but not group assessments, and only one group set per assessment.²⁴ The upload route turns grade changes on non-posted grades into overrides in your gradebook, and the file only accepts CSV or tab-delimited formats, which makes it a poor fit for anything with a rubric.²⁵ AVA is only as available as your license.

Here is the same example in words, so you can swap in your own numbers. **By hand,** each submission takes 1.5 minutes to open and find, 6 minutes to read and mark up, 5 minutes to type comments and 1.5 minutes to score and enter: 14 minutes, or 14 hours for 60. **With the Ultra tools,** navigation drops to 0.5 minutes, reading and annotating stays at 6, the rubric pass with short criterion notes takes 3, and a spoken overall comment takes 1.5: 11 minutes per submission, or 11 hours, plus 40 minutes of setup and 5 minutes to post all grades. That is 11.75 hours. All numbers here are an EXAMPLE, not a measurement.

The honest reading of the example: Ultra's own tools trim the work around the paper, not the paper. If you want a larger step down, the lever is the 6 minutes of reading and the 5 of commenting, and that is where the AI section at the end comes in.

## How do you grade assignments with multiple attempts?

Set the number of attempts in the assignment settings, then choose which attempt counts, because feedback on every attempt shows to students when you post.⁶ ¹¹ In the Grading & Submissions section, Attempts allowed sets the number, Attempts to grade chooses what appears as Needs Grading (First attempt, Last attempt or All attempts), and Final calculation applies when you choose All attempts.¹²

| Final calculation option | When it fits | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Average of all attempts | Several short checkpoints of equal weight | Default grading view shows the most recent attempt¹¹ |
| First attempt with a grade | Diagnostic first draft, no credit for revisions | Later feedback does not change the grade |
| Attempt with highest grade | Reward improvement | Final grade can change as you grade more attempts¹¹ |
| Last attempt with a grade | Outline, bibliography, rough draft, final paper | Only the final paper sets the grade |
| Attempt with lowest grade | Rarely used | Students may read it as punishing revision |

*Table 3: Final calculation options for multiple attempts, from Grade Multiple Attempts. The watch-for notes are ours.*

Anthology's own example is a research paper with four attempts (outline, bibliography, rough draft, final paper) where you give feedback at each stage but only the final paper counts.¹¹ Two rules to remember. When you post, students can see feedback on every attempt, including ungraded ones. And you can only override the final grade, not each attempt's grade, and an override takes precedence over attempts students submit later.¹¹ ²⁰

## How do you grade anonymously, and which grading modes can you combine?

Turn on Hide student names in the assignment settings before any student submits, because Ultra does not let you change the setting afterward.¹⁴ Names are hidden while you grade and are revealed only when you post all grades, which is irreversible.¹⁵ Submissions appear as "Anonymous Student" with a number, in random order, and you can sort by grade to review papers near a boundary before you post.¹⁵

Step 3 "You grade: names show as Anonymous Student + number, random order, sortable by grade" (blue). Step 4 "Post all grades: names revealed, cannot be undone" (red marker). Step 5 "After reveal: you can still update posted grades and grade late submissions" (grey). A note under step 3: "A name inside the student's file defeats anonymity".]
*Figure 5: Anonymity is set before submission and ends at one irreversible click, so plan any grade review before you post.*

Anonymous grading has limits. The Anonymous Grading page says it is not available for group assignments or delegated grading, although the Delegated Grading page says delegated grading supports anonymous submissions, so check that combination with your administrator before you rely on it. A student who writes their name inside the document also undoes the anonymity.¹³ ²⁴ It also does not work with Post assessment grades automatically or Peer review.¹⁴ If your institution has turned it on, authorized users can reveal one student's identity for one submission through the ellipsis menu, and the identity re-hides after logout, timeout or refresh.¹⁵

| Grading mode | Use it when | Does not work with |
|---|---|---|
| Anonymous grading | The work is subjective or several graders mark it | Group assignments, automatic posting, peer review; delegated grading is disputed in Anthology's pages¹³ ¹⁴ ²⁴ |
| Parallel grading | You want two independent opinions and a final reconciler | Group work; questions in the assessment²³ |
| Delegated grading | A large class is split among TAs or graders | Group assessments; more than one group set per assessment²⁴ |

*Table 4: Three grading modes and their documented limits. In parallel grading, graders cannot see each other's grades, feedback, annotations or rubrics, and only the reconciler's rubric and annotations reach students.⁶ ²³*

## What about group assignments, discussions and tests?

Each of these has its own grading page, and the differences are small but easy to trip over. Group grading gives everyone the same grade by default, discussions grade total contributions rather than single posts, and tests can be graded by question instead of by student.

[CARDS: 3]
- **Group assignments** | From the Submissions page, select a group name. Add feedback on the Group tab and per student on the Individual tab. Typing a different grade for one member labels it Override. You cannot change grade settings or group membership after you start grading.¹⁶
- **Discussions** | Grades come from a student's total contributions, not each post. Open the Grades & Participation tab, select a name, and grade with a rubric if you attached one. Discussion analysis is there to inform you, and Anthology says it cannot replace reading responses.¹⁷
- **Tests by question** | Select a test, then the Questions tab on the left, and grade each question across all students. This is aimed at essay questions. It applies to tests, not to file-upload assignments.⁴
[/CARDS]

## How do you post grades, and why can't students see them?

Post the grade: until you do, students see neither grade nor feedback, and for assignments Ultra does not post automatically unless you select Post assessment grades automatically in the assessment settings.¹⁸ That setting is off by default for assignments and on by default for new tests.¹⁸

You can post from the base navigation Grades page, the grid view in the Gradebook, or the Submissions page.¹⁸ In the grid, the paper airplane icon under an item posts every grade for that item at once, with a confirmation dialog before anything goes out.³ The Needs Posting icon at the top of a column tells you when graded work is sitting unposted.³

Yes → blue box "Names and grades are released only after Post all grades; check that you posted all". No → Q3 "Is it a test with question scores or submission view timed to release later?" Tests have extra timing controls. For tests, question scores are by default available after all grades are posted, and you can time the release of submission views, question scores and correct answers, for example after an individual grade is posted, after the due date, after all grades are posted, or on a specific date.¹⁹ So a student who can see the grade but not the answers is usually seeing the Assessment results settings working as designed.

## What are the common grading problems in Ultra, and how do you fix them?

Most problems fall into three groups: grades that students cannot see, late work, and overrides that do something you did not expect. The table gives the likely cause and the fix, each from Anthology's help.

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Grade shows but status still Needs Grading | You entered a grade as an override on an item with a rubric¹ ⁶ | Use Regrade with the rubric, or Undo Override⁶ ²⁰ |
| Student sees feedback on an ungraded attempt | Posting releases feedback for all attempts¹¹ | Keep private remarks in Grader Notes, not in the feedback box⁴ |
| Late work is accepted | Ultra accepts and marks late submissions by default²¹ | Select Prohibit Late Submissions, or grant an exception with extra attempts or extended access¹⁸ ²¹ |
| Late work you do not want to grade | Student can submit late and update the grade | Automatic Zeros in Gradebook Settings; students can still submit late²² |
| Rubric added after grading began | Existing grades became overrides | Select Regrade with the rubric in Rubric Details⁶ |
| Annotations missing in a PDF | Browser's built-in viewer | Open in Adobe Acrobat⁵ |
| Cannot grade a student who has a draft | Attempt saved, not submitted | Submit draft attempt on their behalf¹ |

*Table 5: Frequent problems, their causes and their fixes.*

On late work, Ultra accepts and marks late submissions after the due date unless you prohibit them, and a late timestamp appears in red with a "late" label.¹ ²¹ Prohibit Late Submissions auto-submits in-progress and saved attempts at the due date and sends students a receipt by email, and specified accommodations are still honored.²¹ Anthology lists several settings that do not combine with it, including group submissions, peer review, offline submissions and LTI tools.²¹ If you use an external LTI tool inside an assignment, test the combination in a sandbox course.

On overrides, an override replaces the calculated score and takes precedence over all other grade entries, including attempts submitted later.²⁰ If you override a grade on an item with a rubric, the rubric grade pill shows the override and you can select regrade with the rubric to return to rubric scoring.²⁰ You can also override the overall grade in the Gradebook, and the cell shades gray until you select Undo Override.²⁰ Overridden grades can carry their own feedback through the Grade & Feedback panel.²⁰

For disputes, two records help. The Attempt Logs report lists start and submission times and the submission receipt number for assignments.²⁹ Pasting a receipt number into the gradebook search bar opens the matching submission.²⁸

> **For program directors**
> Ultra gives each instructor good tools and no shared standard by default. If several part-time instructors grade the same assessment, decide four things in writing: which rubric, whether grading is anonymous, who reconciles disagreements, and when grades post. Without those, the same assignment is graded differently in each section and nobody can say why.

## Where does AI fit when you grade in Blackboard Ultra, and what does Eduface add?

Blackboard's own AI tools help you write and tidy feedback you have already decided on; Eduface drafts a rubric-based score and feedback for each submission, and you approve it before the grade returns to the Blackboard gradebook.⁸ ²⁶ That is a different job: it targets the reading and commenting time that the Ultra tools in the example above leave alone.

On the Blackboard side, the inputs Anthology lists for the Summarize option are the rubric criteria, the performance levels you selected, their descriptions, your criterion feedback and any text already in Overall Feedback.⁸ The submission itself is not among the listed inputs, so it is a drafting aid for your own judgment. The third post in this series, on [AI grading in Blackboard Ultra](/resources/blog/blackboard-ultra-ai-grading), goes through that in detail.

Eduface connects to Blackboard through LTI 1.3. The one-time administrator setup is covered in our [Blackboard integration guide](/resources/blog/ai-essay-grader-blackboard-lti), and we do not repeat it here. After setup, the instructor works from inside Blackboard to open Eduface, reviews what Eduface suggests in the Eduface interface, and approves it. Only then does the grade return to the Blackboard gradebook through LTI Grade Services. Students do not need a separate login.²⁶

## What should you do next?

Pick one assignment and make five changes before the next batch arrives. Each takes less than half an hour and none needs your administrator.

1. **Attach a rubric before the first submission.** Adding one after grading starts turns existing grades into overrides, and you will need to regrade.⁶ Build it using the steps in our [rubric guide](/resources/blog/how-to-create-and-use-a-rubric-in-blackboard-ultra).
2. **Load the Content Library.** Write the eight to ten comments you type most and add them before you open the first paper. If your hosting does not include it, use a snippet file.
3. **Decide the feedback channel for each kind of comment.** Use Figure 3, and test one 60-second audio comment on a single paper to see whether it is faster for you.
4. **Time the first five papers.** Record minutes for opening, reading, commenting and scoring. The slowest segment is your next target.
5. **Check how and when you post.** Confirm whether the assignment posts automatically, then decide to post all grades once at the end.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I grade an assignment in Blackboard Ultra?**
Open the course Gradebook, select the Needs Grading icon under the assignment, choose a student on the Submissions page, then grade in Flexible Grading with Bb Annotate, a rubric and the feedback panel. Select Post Grades to release the grade, because assignments do not post automatically by default.³ ⁴ ¹⁸

**Can I grade by question in Blackboard Ultra?**
Yes, for tests. Select a test, open the Questions tab on the left of the Submissions page, and the center panel shows every student's answer to one question, which suits essay questions. It does not apply to assignments with file uploads.⁴

**Can I save comments and reuse them in Blackboard Ultra?**
Yes, in one place. The Content Library in Bb Annotate stores reusable comments you can search and place on a submission, and it is only available in SaaS environments.⁵ We found no equivalent documented for the overall feedback box or rubric criterion boxes, so a text file or a text-expander app is the common workaround.

**How long can audio feedback be in Blackboard Ultra?**
Five minutes without a Video Studio license and 20 minutes with one. You open the recorder with Audio/Video in the feedback panel, and Anthology recommends Chrome or Firefox for embedded media.⁴ ⁷

**Why can't my students see their grades in Blackboard Ultra?**
Because the grade is not posted yet, in most cases. Assignments do not post automatically unless you select Post assessment grades automatically, and anonymous assessments release identities and grades only after you post all grades. Tests can also hold question scores until a set time.¹³ ¹⁸ ¹⁹

**Can I use Eduface to grade assignments inside Blackboard Ultra?**
Yes. Eduface connects to Blackboard Ultra through LTI 1.3, and approved grades return to the Blackboard gradebook through LTI Grade Services. The instructor reviews and approves every suggestion first, and the administrator setup is described in our Blackboard integration guide.²⁶

**Does Blackboard Ultra have AI grading?**
Not for student submissions, as far as Anthology's instructor help for AVA shows. Its AI feedback tools draft and polish the instructor's own feedback from a rubric you completed, and Anthology's administrator help says an AVA license is required after June 30, 2026.⁸ ⁹ Our post on [AI grading in Blackboard Ultra](/resources/blog/blackboard-ultra-ai-grading) covers this in full.
