# How to Buy AI Assessment Tools Through the HEAnet Framework

*By Eduface Team · July 2026 · 8 min read*

Our companion piece on the Jisc/CHEST framework covers procurement for UK institutions. This is the equivalent for Ireland, and it's worth its own piece rather than a footnote, because HEAnet's process has real differences worth knowing before you start, not after.

## What HEAnet actually is

HEAnet is Ireland's national education and research network, providing shared services, including procurement frameworks, to universities, institutes of technology, and other publicly funded education and research bodies across the country. For a training provider or institution evaluating an AI assessment tool, buying through an established HEAnet framework, where one exists for the relevant category, generally means a shorter procurement cycle than running a full public tender independently, since the framework has already done the heavy lifting on baseline compliance and terms.

## Why this matters more than it might seem

Public procurement in Ireland, as in most EU member states, is bound by procurement thresholds and competitive tendering obligations that can turn a straightforward software purchase into a months-long process if approached from scratch. A framework agreement exists precisely to avoid that: suppliers are vetted once, at the framework level, and individual institutions can then call off from the framework without re-running the full competitive process every time. For AI assessment tools specifically, where the vendor landscape is moving quickly and institutions often want to move from evaluation to pilot in weeks rather than a full academic year, that difference is not a minor convenience.

## What to check before assuming a tool is covered

Not every AI assessment vendor sits on every relevant HEAnet framework, and framework coverage can be narrower than a vendor's marketing implies, sometimes covering general software categories without specifically addressing the assessment or AI-in-education use case. Before assuming procurement will be straightforward, confirm three things directly: whether the specific framework lot covers AI-assisted assessment tools as a category, whether the vendor is an approved supplier under that specific lot rather than a broader, less relevant one, and what the call-off procedure actually requires from your institution, since this varies between frameworks and can still involve a mini-competition even when the supplier is pre-approved.

## The EU dimension that UK equivalents don't have

Because Ireland is an EU member state, procurement of an AI assessment tool through HEAnet sits inside a regulatory context that UK institutions procuring through Jisc do not share in the same way. The EU AI Act classifies AI systems used to evaluate learning outcomes as high risk, which brings obligations around risk management, data governance, and documented human oversight that should be part of any procurement due diligence, not an afterthought handled separately from the buying decision. A vendor's ability to demonstrate EU AI Act compliance, alongside GDPR compliance more broadly, is worth treating as a procurement question in its own right, not just a legal one to be resolved after signature.

## A practical procurement checklist

Confirm the specific framework and lot, not just that the vendor is "on a HEAnet framework" in general terms. Ask for evidence of EU AI Act risk classification and what safeguards are in place given that classification. Confirm data residency, since where student data is actually processed and stored matters under both GDPR and your own institution's data protection policies. Ask how the tool handles the human oversight requirement in practice, not just as a compliance claim, our companion piece on human-in-the-loop marking sets out what a genuine version of that oversight needs to look like. And confirm what the call-off procedure requires from your side before treating framework membership as equivalent to a done deal.

## FAQ

**Is buying through HEAnet always faster than an independent tender?**
Generally yes, where a relevant framework and lot exist, since the baseline supplier vetting has already happened. It's not automatic, though, some call-off procedures still require a mini-competition between framework suppliers.

**Does EU AI Act compliance apply differently to Irish institutions than UK ones?**
Yes. Ireland is an EU member state, so the EU AI Act applies directly. UK institutions are not bound by it in the same way, though many vendors serving both markets build to the stricter standard regardless.

**What's the biggest mistake institutions make in this procurement process?**
Assuming a vendor's general claim of framework approval covers the specific lot relevant to AI assessment tools, without confirming that directly.

**Does framework approval remove the need to check human oversight and data residency separately?**
No. Framework approval covers baseline supplier vetting. Data residency, EU AI Act risk classification, and genuine human oversight are still worth confirming directly as part of your own due diligence.
