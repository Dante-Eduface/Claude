## Post 1 · Dante · P1 Staat en toekomst van toetsen · EN

Universal Technical Institute wants to open two new campuses a year through 2029, on the way to a billion dollars in revenue.

The students are there. Enrollment at vocational and trade-focused institutions is up almost 20% since 2020, and reached 871,000 this spring, according to the @National Student Clearinghouse Research Center.

The instructors are the harder part.

UTI runs 35 campuses with around 1,500 instructors. Their own annual filing lists hiring and keeping people as a risk to the business, which is unusually straight for a 10-K.

And what makes it hard is not what most people assume. 57% of schools say recruiting CTE instructors is difficult, against 39% for academic subjects. A 2024 study in Educational Researcher found that the instructors most likely to leave teaching are the ones who earn the most after they leave. The better someone is at the trade, the cheaper it is for them to walk.

So you are not really competing with other colleges for instructors. You are competing with the industry your graduates are going into.

You cannot always win that on pay. What you can change is what the job feels like day to day. Someone who came out of a workshop to teach the trade did not come in to spend their evenings marking. That part of the job is a variable, and it is one of the few you can move without touching the salary line.

Growing this fast while the people qualified to teach it have somewhere better to be is a hard problem. Not an impossible one.

If you work in trade or technical education, what actually keeps your good instructors from going back to industry?

## Post 2 · Dante · P3 Pijn feedback & nakijken · EN

Most tools in education do not fail because they are bad. They fail in week one.

Here is how a rollout usually goes. The tool gets integrated into the LMS. You train the module leaders so they can train the instructors. Then you ask every instructor to spend an hour getting used to it, and a couple more fitting it into how they already work.

For some of them that is a way of working they have had for ten years. In trade and technical schools it is often someone who came out of industry to teach, not a career academic, and configuration screens are not what they signed up for.

So the tool sits there. A year later somebody puts the cost next to the usage and it gets swapped for something else. Not because it did not work. Because it never got started.

@Tyton Partners found the same pattern in Time for Class 2025. The biggest constraints on faculty using digital tools are time and training, not resistance. And among faculty using AI, the daily users report less workload while the occasional users report more.

That last bit is the part I keep coming back to. Half-adoption is worse than none. You pay for the tool, you pay for the training, and the people who only half use it end up with more work than they started with.

What we have learned is that the step that decides adoption is the first one, setting the thing up. So we took it off the instructor. One button, and the course pulls in what is already in the LMS, the slides, the brief, the rubric, the handbook, and builds itself from there.

They open it and it is ready.

If you have rolled something out across a faculty, what got people through that first session?

## Wat ik bewust NIET gebruikt heb

- **"Instructeurs verdienen meer in het werkveld"** (jouw oorspronkelijke haak). Klopt niet op BLS-medianen
  mei 2025: postsecondary CTE-docent $72.060 versus lassers $53.750, elektriciens $63.190, dieselmonteurs
  $61.770, HVAC $68.120. Het echte mechanisme zit in de bovenste staart en in het Educational
  Researcher-onderzoek: wie het meest kan verdienen na vertrek, vertrekt het eerst. Dat staat nu in post 1.
- **Learnosity-cijfer over nakijkdruk** (9,9 uur per week, een derde overwoog te stoppen). Vendor-onderzoek
  en K-12, niet postsecondary CTE. Te zwak als dragend bewijs.
- **"27% van edtech-licenties wordt nooit gebruikt."** Uit 2019, niet representatief, wordt overal
  gerecycled alsof het actueel is. Tyton 2025 is de betere onderbouwing voor hetzelfde punt.
- **Alles uit het gesprek van 18-09 met UTI.** De scherpste regels zaten daar (instructeurspay als grootste
  variabele post die een campus stuurt, instructeurs die het land door verhuisd worden, "they can't tutor
  because they're locked into grading"). Dat is niet openbaar en de deal loopt nog.
