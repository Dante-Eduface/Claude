# Answer key ISU-18, ISU-19, ISU-20
Source: Solution and Answer Guide, Silberstein/Obrzut, The Complete HVACR Manual 2E, Section 13: Installation and Start-Up (ISU), pages 27-28.

## Exercise ISU-18

Q: What pulls the refrigerant to the sensor in a halide leak detector?
A: The primary air for the torch.

Q: What is the normal color of the gas flame in a halide leak detector?
A: Blue.

Q: What is the color of the gas flame in a halide leak detector when refrigerant is present?
A: Green.

Q: Can a halide leak detector be used to detect natural gas? Why?
A: No, an explosion would occur because the halide detector has an open flame.

Q: What does the term halide refer to?
A: The halogenated hydrocarbon series of refrigerants.

Q: Name two gases that are commonly used to produce the flame in a halide leak detector.
A: Acetylene and propane.

Q: Describe how a halide leak detector discovers leaks.
A: A refrigerant sample from the leak is pulled into the sample tube by the primary air for the burner. The sample of refrigerant passes by a glowing copper element and is broken down by the heat, which changes the flame color to green.

Q: Does the halide leak detector work best in the light or in the dark?
A: Dark, because you can see the blue flame better.

Q: Why will a halide leak detector not be able to locate a leak on an R-410A air-conditioning system?
A: The halide leak detector relies on the presence of chlorine to change the flame color. R-410A is an HFC blend and contains no chlorine.

Q: How small of a leak can a halide leak detector typically pick up?
A: As small as about 1 oz of refrigerant per year.

## Exercise ISU-19

Q: Can natural gas leaks be located with an electronic leak detector?
A: No, ignition may occur.

Q: How can the refrigerant be moved from the point of the leak to the detector sensor?
A: With a small pump to provide a suction in the electronic leak detector.

Q: What can be done to keep wind from affecting leak detection?
A: Shield the area where you are attempting to find a leak.

Q: What is the power source for an electronic leak detector?
A: Batteries or 115 V AC.

Q: How can soap bubbles be used to further pinpoint the leak when it is detected?
A: Soap bubbles will form a bubble at the point of the leak.

Q: What is the purpose of the filter under the tip of the sensor on the detector?
A: To prevent airborne particles from being sucked into the sensing element.

Q: Does the electronic leak detector work best in a light or dark place?
A: Either.

Q: How can an electronic leak detector be used to pinpoint the source of a large leak?
A: Most models have a sensitivity adjustment that can be used when there is a large leak. The sensitivity can be turned down to prevent the alarm from going off before it reaches the source of the leak.

## Exercise ISU-20

Q: After a hermetic motor burn, what contaminant might be found in the recovered refrigerant?
A: Acid.

Q: What would the result be if a recovery cylinder were filled with liquid and allowed to warm up?
A: The pressure would increase and it could possibly explode.

Q: Why must liquid and vapor refrigerant be present for the pressure/temperature relationship to apply?
A: There must be liquid present to boil if the temperature rises.

Q: If an air-conditioning unit using R-22 were located totally outside and the temperature were 85°F, what would the pressure in the system be with the unit off? (Hint: It would follow the P and T relationship.)
A: 155.7 psig.
