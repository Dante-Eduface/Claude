## The goal
**Lean bulk toward a Greek god V-taper.** Add muscle slowly enough that bodyfat stays near 10-12%. He is already lean (10.2% at 89.4 kg), so there is no reason to bulk hard. Bodyfat lands on the waist first, and the waist is half the shoulder-to-waist ratio he is chasing.

### Where 3,100 comes from, and why to distrust it
- Measured BMR (InBody): 2,104 kcal. That matches Katch-McArdle on 80.3 kg lean mass almost exactly, so trust it.
- Activity multiplier ~1.5 (desk job, daily commute walking, 4 gym sessions + field day): TDEE ~3,150.
- WHOOP says he burns **2,307 cal/day** over the last 90 days. WHOOP underestimates total burn, so this is a floor, not the truth.

So 3,100 is maintenance-ish with a small surplus. **Steer on the scale, not the calculator.**

## The rule that beats every calculation

**Weigh-in and InBody every Sunday.** Same conditions every time: morning, after the toilet, before eating, before training. Log both.

A single weekly weigh-in is noisy. Water, glycogen and yesterday's food move the scale 0.5-1 kg, and the signal you're looking for is only ~0.25 kg/week. So **never react to one week.**

- **Judge on a 3-week trend.** A correct lean bulk shows roughly **+0.6 to +0.9 kg over 3 weeks.**
- More than +1.2 kg in 3 weeks: too fast, that is fat. Drop 200 kcal.
- Flat or down over 3 weeks: add 200 kcal.
- One weird week on its own means nothing. Wait for the next one.

**The InBody is the better signal, use it first.** Because he scans weekly, bodyfat % and skeletal muscle mass tell him what the scale can't: whether the gain is muscle or fat. Weight up + bodyfat % flat or down = it is working, ignore the scale number. Weight up + bodyfat % climbing = cut 200 kcal even if the weight gain looks correct.

- **Bodyfat % is the veto.** Past ~12%, stop adding calories regardless of anything else.
- Waist tape every 4-6 weeks alongside it. Past 80 cm, same veto.

## Protein
175-180 g is the non-negotiable. Practical version: **4 feeds of ~45 g**, spread across the day. He already logs "Consumed protein" yes on 20 of 21 days in WHOOP, so the habit exists, the question is whether the number is actually there.

Hitting 45 g four times means real portions, not shakes standing in for meals. Rough anchors: 150 g chicken ~45 g, 200 g quark ~24 g, 4 eggs ~26 g, one scoop whey ~24 g.

## Around training
Sessions are Tue/Thu evening and Sat/Sun daytime.

- **Evening sessions (Tue, Thu):** real carbs at lunch and again 2 h before the gym. This is the half of the week where he is training on a workday after a 6:23 train.
- **Weekend sessions (Sat, Sun):** train later, eat a proper meal beforehand rather than training fasted.
- **Post-session:** protein feed within a couple of hours. The 30-minute anabolic window is not real, the daily total is.
- **Sunday** carries Lower B plus field day. That is the highest-calorie day of the week. Do not undereat it.

## Sleep and eating (this is the priority)
Sleep, not calories, is the limiter right now: **6.14 h average, 3 of the last 23 nights hit 8 h.** A lean bulk on 6 hours of sleep partitions worse, and recovery already sits at 57%.

- WHOOP logs "Ate food close to bedtime" **yes on only 3 of 21 days**, so late eating is not the problem. Good.
- The 6:23 train forces a ~05:15 wake. To get 8 h he has to be asleep by 21:15, which means dinner earlier and the last big carb feed earlier.
- **Sunday night is his worst night of the week at 5.41 h**, right after the heaviest training day. Front-load Sunday's food and finish eating early.

## How he actually eats

- **Cooks and preps every Sunday**, 2 to 2,5 hours. The whole week is decided on Sunday.
- **Does not track.** No calorie logging, no weighing food, no app. He wants a plan, not a diary.
- Takes lunch and snacks to the Utrecht office. Everything has to work cold or from a microwave.
- **Hard dislikes: no feta, no walnuts, no chili, no sambal.**
- Has a full recipe book he is bored of. Chicken thigh in 3 of 6 mains, passata in 4, 800 g kwark a day. Rotating this is a real job, not a nice-to-have. See `recepten.md`.

## The weekly rhythm

**Sunday is the hinge.** Train (Upper B + field day), weigh in, InBody, prep the food, and review the week.

The review is the only feedback loop, and it is loose on purpose: he says what he ate, what he skipped, and what he added. No numbers. That plus the InBody is enough to steer on.

## The weekly menu (this is the deliverable, not a tracker)

Every Sunday he gets a week overview: breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks per day, with the prep order and a shopping list. Rules for building it, in `recepten.md`:

- Max 2x the same protein source and 2x the same sauce base per week.
- Dinner is never the same dish as that day's lunch.
- Kwark once a day, not twice.
- **Front-load the day.** Breakfast and lunch are the big meals, dinner is normal, done eating by 19:30. That protects the 21:15 bedtime the 6:23 train demands, and sleep is the bottleneck for the whole plan.
- **Tuesday and Thursday need a pre-gym feed around 17:00** at the office. Those are evening sessions after a workday, and without it he trains at 19:00 on a 12:30 lunch.
- Sunday is the biggest day of the week (Upper B + field day). Do not undereat it.
- Rest days (Mon, Wed, Fri): same protein, slightly fewer carbs, never below ~2.800 kcal.
