Q (Summary statement): Explain the differences between an absolute temperature scale, such as the Rankine scale, and a non-absolute temperature scale, such as the Fahrenheit scale. In your response, be sure to include the significance of the low-end values of the scales.
A: Kelvin and Rankine are absolute scales; Fahrenheit and Celsius are not. On an absolute scale, zero is absolute zero, so the scale has no negative values: 0°R and 0 K are the same point as −460°F and −273°C. A non-absolute scale puts its zero at another point (0°C is 32°F, 492°R and 273 K) and uses negative values below it, down to −460°F and −273°C at absolute zero.
