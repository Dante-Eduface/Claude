# Answer key Exercise RSC-7: Air-Cooled Condenser Performance
Source: Solution and Answer Guide, Silberstein/Obrzut, The Complete HVACR Manual 2E, Section 3: Refrigeration System Components (RSC), and the RSC-7 lab sheet.

## Context
- Objectives: Upon completion of this exercise, you should be able to perform basic evaluation procedures on an air-cooled condenser.
- Introduction: You will use a thermometer and a gauge manifold to calculate subcooling and the condenser split (CS) of the system. A typical split is about 30° but can vary depending on the type of unit and its efficiency.
- Takeaway: When evaluating a system component, such as the condenser, it is important for technicians to gather as much information about its operating conditions as possible. Subcooling and condenser split are two important calculations done when executing an evaluation of an air-cooled condenser.

Q (Summary statement): Describe how condenser split and subcooling can be used to evaluate the operation of a condenser.
A: Condenser split is the condenser saturation temperature minus the condenser entering air temperature. A typical split is about 30°F, but it can vary depending on the type of unit and its efficiency; a split above 30°F points to a problem such as a dirty condenser or recirculated air. Subcooling is the condenser saturation temperature minus the liquid line temperature and shows how far the liquid has been cooled below the condensing temperature, which is one of the three functions of the condenser. Together, the two calculations give information about the operating conditions of the condenser.

Q (1): Name the three functions that a condenser performs.
A: Desuperheats, condenses, and subcools the refrigerant.

Q (2): What is considered the highest temperature that the discharge gas should reach upon leaving the compressor?
A: Approximately 210°F.

Q (3): What may be used on an air-cooled condenser with a fan to prevent the head pressure from becoming too low in mild weather?
A: A low-ambient control to cycle the fan.

Q (4): What effects would low head pressure have on a system with an air-cooled condenser?
A: The evaporator is starved because the pressure drop through the expansion device is too small for the correct flow.

Q (5): Why must an air-cooled condenser be located where the air will not recirculate through it?
A: Recirculated air causes high head pressure because the air is warmer.

Q (6): What is the difference between a natural draft and a mechanical draft condenser?
A: A natural draft condenser has no fan, it gives up heat to the surrounding area, a mechanical draft condenser has a fan that either pulls or forces air through the condenser to ensure heat transfer.

Q (7): What are the symptoms of a dirty condenser?
A: Typical: High suction and discharge pressures, low superheat and subcooling, and high compressor amperage.

Q (8): What is the purpose of the fins on the condenser?
A: To increase the surface area of the tubes to improve condenser heat transfer.

Q (9): What materials are typical refrigeration condensers made of?
A: Copper, aluminum, and steel.
