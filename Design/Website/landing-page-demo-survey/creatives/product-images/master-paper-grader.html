Is consideration still necessary for a binding contract?

2,140 words

The doctrine of consideration requires that each party gives something of value in exchange for a promise. Without it, an agreement is not enforceable, however clearly it was intended. This essay argues that the doctrine has been hollowed out by the courts to the point where it no longer performs its original function.

2. The practical benefit test

In Williams v Roffey Bros the Court of Appeal held that a promise to pay more for the same work was enforceable, because the promisor obtained a practical benefit. This shows that consideration can now be found in almost any commercial situation. Critics argue that the decision left the doctrine without clear limits.
